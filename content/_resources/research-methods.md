## Designing & conducting experiments

**Designing and running human factors experiments:**  Bjoern recommends Jake Wobbrock’s (UW) and Scott Klemmer’s (UCSD) [MOOC on this topic,](https://www.mooc-list.com/course/designing-running-and-analysing-experiments-coursera) as well as the book [*Measuring the User Experience*](http://oskicat.berkeley.edu/search~S1?/tMeasuring+the+User+Experience%2C+Second+Edition%3A+Collecting%2C+Analyzing%2C+and+Presenting+Usability+Metrics+/tmeasuring+the+user+experience+second+edition+collecting+analyzing+and+presenting+usability+metrics/-3%2C0%2C0%2CB/exact&FF=tmeasuring+the+user+experience+collecting+analyzing+and+presenting+usability+metrics&1%2C3%2C/indexsort=-) (free electronic resource on OskiCat; print version available)

**Statistical hypothesis testing:**  Wikipedia has a pretty good [overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing) of analyzing interventions to see if a tool or methodology does or does not provide some particular benefit to the student. Also see Koji Yatani’s [course pages on “Stats for HCI”](http://yatani.jp/teaching/doku.php?id=hcistats:start#ChooseTest), including a cheat sheet for “which statistical test should I use for…”

**For evaluating tools specifically**, a few valuable resources:

- The [System Usability Scale](https://www.usability.gov/how-to-and-tools/methods/system-usability-scale.html): a “quick and dirty” reliable tool for measuring tool usability
- NASA TLX ([task load index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA-TLX)): a widely used, subjective, multidimensional assessment tool that rates perceived workload in order to assess a task, system, or team’s effectiveness or other aspects of performance
- [A practical guide to controlled experiments of software engineering tools with human participants](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/s10664-013-9279-3), by Amy J. Ko et al. Aimed primarily at tools supporting software engineering tasks and processes.
